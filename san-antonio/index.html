Housing in and around San Antonio

Historic neighborhoods

Monte Vista, Tobin Hill, King William and other older districts have homes from the late 1800s and early 1900s, many on pier-and-beam foundations, with bathrooms that were added or remodeled over the decades. Measurements can be surprising, and historic district rules may apply to exterior work. Interior bathroom changes rarely trigger historic review, but ask the city if you are unsure.

Postwar and ranch-style homes

Large parts of the city and inner suburbs were built from the 1950s through the 1980s as single-story homes. These are often well suited to aging in place, and their hall baths often accept a compact walk-in tub or a low-threshold shower.

Newer suburbs

Growth to the north toward Boerne and New Braunfels and to the west toward Helotes has brought newer slab homes, some on rocky Hill Country lots. Two-story plans mean checking stairs and floor framing before an upstairs install.

Water and weather

Much of the region draws on the Edwards Aquifer, and hard water is common. Scale builds on fixtures, seals and jets over time, so ask about cleaning routines and consider a softener if you already fight buildup. The area also sees flash flooding along creeks during heavy rain, scorching summers, and the occasional hard freeze.

Military City veterans

San Antonio is home to Joint Base San Antonio and a very large community of military retirees and veterans. The South Texas Veterans Health Care System, including the Audie L. Murphy Memorial Veterans Hospital, serves the region. Veterans can ask their VA provider about the HISA grant for bathroom changes, and those with severe service-connected disabilities may qualify for SAH or SHA housing grants. The Texas Veterans Commission helps with claims. See help for Texas veterans.

Local help for older adults

  • Bexar Area Agency on Aging for Bexar County residents.
  • Alamo Area Agency on Aging for Atascosa, Bandera, Comal, Frio, Gillespie, Guadalupe, Karnes, Kendall, Kerr, McMullen, Medina and Wilson counties.
  • Both are coordinated through the Alamo Area Council of Governments. Statewide line: 1-800-252-9240.
  • 2-1-1 Texas for local repair and assistance programs.

Permits

The City of San Antonio's development services department handles permits inside city limits. Suburban cities such as Schertz, Converse, Live Oak and New Braunfels run their own offices, and unincorporated Bexar County addresses follow county and utility rules. Licensed trades are required for plumbing and electrical work everywhere.

Choosing well

A single-story San Antonio home with a hall bath is an easy fit for either a walk-in tub or a roll-in shower. For a veteran using a wheelchair, a roll-in shower or transfer tub is often the better route, and VA occupational therapists can help decide.

Before you call

Note whether your home is inside San Antonio city limits or in a suburban city, whether it is on a slab or pier-and-beam, and whether hard water has already left scale in your current tub. If a VA team is involved, mention it so we can format the quote the way they need.

Common questions

Which aging agency serves San Antonio?

The Bexar Area Agency on Aging serves Bexar County. The Alamo Area Agency on Aging serves 12 surrounding counties. Call 1-800-252-9240.

Where do San Antonio veterans apply for a HISA grant?

Through the Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service of the South Texas Veterans Health Care System, after a VA provider documents the medical need.

Does hard water affect walk-in tubs?

It can leave scale on seals, fixtures and jet nozzles. Regular cleaning and a softener, if needed, help.
