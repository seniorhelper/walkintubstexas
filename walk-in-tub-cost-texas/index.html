What the published ranges say

Two widely read cost guides give a sense of scale. Fixr reports a national average close to $6,000 for an installed walk-in tub, with the bulk of projects landing from roughly $3,500 up to $10,000. This Old House shows a wider window that runs from around $1,800 for an entry-level unit to beyond $20,000 when a premium model meets a major bathroom rebuild. Both are national, secondary figures. We cite them so you have a yardstick, not as a price from us.

Texas is a big state with very different labor markets. A job in a Dallas suburb, a ranch house outside San Angelo and a condo in South Padre will not cost the same, even with the same tub. The honest answer is that your quote depends on the tub you choose, what your bathroom needs, and where you live.

Texas factors that push the number up or down

The foundation under the bathroom

Most newer Texas homes sit on concrete slabs. When the new tub's drain lands where the old one was, hookup is quick. When it doesn't, concrete gets cut and patched. Older homes in parts of Dallas, Austin and San Antonio often sit on pier-and-beam foundations instead, where a plumber can usually reach the drain from the crawl space. That can make a drain change easier, though old cast-iron or galvanized lines may need replacing once exposed.

Hot water

A walk-in tub holds a lot of water and fills all at once. Many Texas homes have a 40- or 50-gallon tank in the garage or attic. If that tank is small or tired, the quote may include a bigger heater, an in-line tub heater, or a smaller tub. The U.S. Department of Energy's water heater sizing guide is a good primer.

Electrical service

Soaking tubs need no power. Jets, heated backrests and in-line heaters do, usually on dedicated GFCI-protected circuits. Rural homes and older houses may need panel work before new circuits can go in.

Distance

Travel time is real labor. Homes far from a metro may see that reflected in installed pricing, or may be better served by a supply-only order and a local licensed plumber. See our rural Texas guide.

What the walls are hiding

A tub surround that has leaked quietly for years can leave soft drywall or damaged framing. Nobody knows until the old tub is out. Ask how hidden damage would be handled before you sign.

Finish choices

Solid surround panels go up quickly and clean easily. Tile looks custom and takes longer. Both are fine; they are priced differently.

A Texas quote checklist

  • Exact tub model, size, door direction and drain side
  • Options listed one by one: jets, heated seat, in-line heater, quick drain, handheld shower
  • Removal and disposal of the old tub or shower
  • Plumbing scope, including any drain move and the valve type
  • Electrical scope and who holds the license
  • Walls, floor patching and trim
  • Permits expected for your city or county
  • Manufacturer warranty terms and installer labor coverage, in writing

If you might use a program such as a VA grant or a Medicaid home modification benefit, tell us before we quote. Programs usually need an itemized quote and approval before any work starts. See paying for a walk-in tub in Texas.

Common questions

What does a walk-in tub cost in Texas?

National guides give wide ranges: Fixr reports an average near $6,000 with most projects between about $3,500 and $10,000, and This Old House cites roughly $1,800 to over $20,000. A Texas quote depends on the tub, your bathroom and your location.

Is a walk-in tub cheaper on pier-and-beam than on a slab?

Sometimes. A drain change can be easier from a crawl space than through concrete, but older pipes found under pier-and-beam homes may need replacing.

Can I save money with a supply-only tub?

You may, if you already have a licensed plumber you trust. You take on removal, permits and scheduling yourself. We include phone tech support with supply-only orders.
