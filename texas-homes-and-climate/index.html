House and weather notes

What Texas houses and weather mean for a walk-in tub

Texas homes sit on shifting clay, in dry desert air, by the humid coast, and through the occasional hard freeze. Each condition changes a few details of a good install.

Foundations: slab or pier-and-beam

Slab-on-grade foundations are the norm for most Texas homes built in recent decades. Plumbing runs through or under the concrete. The simplest walk-in tub install puts the new drain exactly where the old one was, so we match the tub's drain end to the existing plumbing whenever possible.

Pier-and-beam homes are common in older neighborhoods of Dallas, Fort Worth, Austin and San Antonio, and in many small towns. A crawl space gives a plumber access from below, which can make drain changes easier. It also means checking the floor framing under a heavy filled tub and looking for old cast-iron or galvanized pipe that may be near the end of its life.

Shifting soil

Expansive clay soils across much of Central and North Texas swell after rain and shrink in drought, and foundations can move a little over time. A walk-in tub needs to stay level for its door to seal and its water to drain completely. Careful leveling and support during installation matter, and an access panel, where the layout allows one, makes later service easier.

Freezes

The February 2021 winter storm left many Texas homes with burst pipes, and cities across the state saw a wave of plumbing repairs. If your bathroom shares an exterior wall, or plumbing runs through an unheated space, ask about insulation and pipe placement during the install. Walk-in tubs with water-jet systems hold some water in their lines; follow the maker's guidance on draining and purging. In a freeze, keep the home heated and know where the main shutoff is.

Heat

Garage and attic water heaters are common in Texas. In summer the incoming water is warm and a tub fills comfortably. In winter, the same heater has to work harder, and a deep tub can outrun a small tank. An in-line heater keeps a bath warm once filled, but it does not replace the water heater. The Department of Energy's sizing guidance is useful if you are replacing one.

Hard water

Many Texas water systems, especially in Central and West Texas, deliver hard water. Over time that leaves scale on fixtures, door seals and jet nozzles. Practical steps: choose finishes that are easy to wipe, follow the jet-cleaning cycle, and consider a water softener if scale is already a problem in your home.

Humidity on the coast, dryness in the west

Along the Gulf, from the Coastal Bend to the Valley, humidity encourages mold in bathrooms. A vented exhaust fan and a well-sealed surround matter, and the EPA recommends keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent. In El Paso and the Trans-Pecos, the opposite problem shows up: very dry air is hard on some sealants and gaskets. Either way, inspect the door seal and caulk lines once or twice a year.

Storms and power

Hurricanes on the coast and severe storms inland can cut power for days. A walk-in tub's door, seat and grab bars work without electricity, and most drain by gravity. Jets, heated seats, in-line heaters and electric water heaters do not run. Keep a battery light in the bathroom.

Rural water and septic

Homes on private wells and septic systems need a little extra planning: water pressure, sediment, and how a large tub's drain volume affects the septic system. See rural Texas.

A quick climate checklist

  • Know where your main water shutoff is.
  • Insulate bathroom pipes on exterior walls.
  • Run a vented exhaust fan during and after baths.
  • Check the door seal and caulk every six months.
  • Clean jets on the maker's schedule, more often with hard water.
  • Keep a battery light in the bathroom for outages.

Common questions

Does a pier-and-beam house make walk-in tub installation harder?

Not necessarily. Crawl-space access can make drain work easier, but floor framing and old pipes need checking.

How does hard water affect a walk-in tub?

Scale builds up on seals, fixtures and jet nozzles. Regular cleaning and, if needed, a softener help.

Can a walk-in tub freeze?

Like any plumbing, its lines can be damaged in a hard freeze if the home loses heat. Follow the maker's guidance on draining jet systems and keep the home heated.
