For family caregivers

A Texas caregiver guide to safer bathing

Helping a parent bathe is one of the hardest parts of caregiving. These steps can make it safer for them and easier on you.

Notice the quiet signs

People rarely announce that bathing has become frightening. You may notice fewer baths, sponge baths at the sink, wet towels but a dry tub, a new reluctance to be home alone, or a parent gripping the towel bar to stand. A fall in the bathroom, even a minor one, is a clear signal. The CDC reports that more than one in four adults 65 and older fall each year.

Start the conversation gently

  • Talk about what they want to keep doing, not what they can't.
  • Ask what part is hardest: getting in, sitting down, standing up, or getting out.
  • Offer to try small changes first.
  • Bring in their doctor; a professional's suggestion can feel less personal than a child's.

Low-cost changes to make first

  1. Properly anchored grab bars at the tub entry and by the toilet.
  2. A transfer bench straddling the tub wall, paired with a hand shower on a hose.
  3. Grip strips on the tub floor and a rubber-backed mat on the bathroom floor.
  4. A nightlight path from bedroom to bathroom.
  5. Turn the water heater down to 120°F or less, the NIA's recommendation, to cut scald risk.

When the room needs to change

When all of that is done and bathing is still unsafe, or you find yourself lifting, it's time to consider a bigger change. A walk-in tub suits someone who loves to soak and can manage a small step. A roll-in shower suits someone who uses a wheelchair or needs hands-on help. Our tub or shower guide goes through the choice.

Protect your own body

  • Set up everything before the bath: towels, clothes, soap, shampoo.
  • Test water temperature yourself.
  • Guide and steady; avoid lifting. If lifting is routine, ask the doctor about a home health or therapy referral.
  • Kneel on a pad, not the tile, and keep your back straight.
  • Never leave an unsteady person alone in a full tub.

Texas caregiver support

  • Area Agencies on Aging, 1-800-252-9240: caregiver support, respite and training programs, benefits counseling.
  • Aging and Disability Resource Centers, 1-855-937-2372: help with long-term services and Medicaid questions.
  • 2-1-1 Texas: local programs, transportation and repair help.
  • Texas Long-Term Care Ombudsman: if a parent lives in a nursing home or assisted living and you have concerns.
  • VA Caregiver Support: for veterans' caregivers, through any VA health system.

Long-distance caregivers

Texas is big, and many adult children live hours away. Our team can handle the planning calls with you, email you the photos and written quote, and set the measuring visit for a day when your parent or a trusted neighbor will be home. Tell us who should get updates.

Take care of the caregiver

Caregiving can run for years. Respite care, even a few hours a week, keeps many caregivers going. Your Area Agency on Aging can tell you what respite and caregiver training programs exist in your county. If the bathing routine is causing you pain or injury, say so to your own doctor and to your parent's doctor. A home health aide, a physical therapist, or a different bathroom setup may be the answer. Asking for help is part of doing the job well.

Common questions

When should a parent stop using a standard bathtub?

Once basic aids like grab bars, a bench and a hand shower are not enough, or someone has to lift them, it is time to change the bathroom itself.

Where can Texas caregivers get support?

Area Agencies on Aging (1-800-252-9240), Aging and Disability Resource Centers (1-855-937-2372) and 2-1-1 Texas all offer help or referrals.

Can I arrange a walk-in tub for a parent who lives in another Texas city?

Yes. We plan by phone with you and coordinate the visit with whoever will be at the house.
