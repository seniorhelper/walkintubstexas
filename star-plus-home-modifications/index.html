STAR+PLUS minor home modifications for a safer bathroom

For some Texans on Medicaid, the STAR+PLUS program can pay for bathroom accessibility changes. Here is how it works, what the limits are, what changed in 2026, and how to start. Updated November 8, 2026.

What STAR+PLUS is

STAR+PLUS is Texas Medicaid's managed care program for adults who are 65 or older or who have a disability. Members get medical care and long-term services and supports through a health plan, also called a managed care organization or MCO. A key part of STAR+PLUS is service coordination: a person at the health plan who helps members who need or ask for extra help organize their services.

Some STAR+PLUS members also qualify for home and community-based services (HCBS), which are meant to help people live safely at home instead of in a nursing facility. Minor home modifications are one of those services.

What the handbook allows

The Texas HHS STAR+PLUS Handbook, section 6600, describes minor home modifications. A few points stand out for bathroom projects:

  • Lifetime limit: $7,500 per member, with $300 per year allowed for repairs, replacement or additional modifications after that limit is reached.
  • Bathroom changes are a recognized category. Related HHS handbook language lists modification of bathroom facilities, ramps and doorway widening among allowable work.
  • Exclusions: the handbook rules out items that are general repairs, such as roofs, gutters or a leaky faucet, and some larger items such as elevators. Additions that create new square footage are not allowed.
  • Above the limit: the handbook describes situations where a plan may exceed the cost limit when the member's service plan documents the need. That is the plan's decision.

Handbooks are revised. Before relying on any number here, ask your service coordinator for the current rule and read the linked section.

What changed in 2026?

Update, November 8, 2026. Three things moved this year. Only one of them touches the bathroom rules on this page, and the dollar cap is not it.

The Dual Demonstration ended on December 31, 2025

Texas HHSC ended the Dual-Eligible Integrated Care Demonstration Project on Dec. 31, 2025, as a federal CMS rule required. The Medicare-Medicaid Plans (MMPs) that ran it began operating as Integrated Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans (D-SNPs) on Jan. 1, 2026 in Bexar, Dallas, El Paso, Harris and Hidalgo counties: Molina in all five, Superior in Dallas and Hidalgo, UnitedHealthcare in Harris. A full dual-eligible adult who picks an Integrated D-SNP for Medicare is automatically enrolled in the related STAR+PLUS plan for Medicaid, with one ID card and one handbook. The practical effect for a bathroom project: if you were in an MMP, your minor home modification request now runs through your STAR+PLUS plan's service coordinator under the handbook rules described here. Statewide, dual-eligible members choose Original Medicare, a Medicare Advantage plan or a D-SNP alongside STAR+PLUS.

Handbook revision 26-3, effective August 14, 2026

On Aug. 14, 2026, HHSC issued revision 26-3 to the STAR+PLUS Program Support Unit Operational Procedures Handbook, the staff manual that sits beside the member-facing STAR+PLUS Handbook. It is a miscellaneous-changes revision: eligibility sections on citizenship, residency and medical necessity determinations, new notification requirements for health plans and program support staff, interest list and enrollment procedures, state fair hearing wording and several retired appendices. It does not change section 6600. As of this update the STAR+PLUS Handbook's own revision list ends at 25-3, the cost limit in section 6630 still carries revision 20-2 (effective Oct. 1, 2020) and the timelines in section 6650 carry revision 21-1 (effective May 1, 2021).

The cap did not move, and $10,000 belongs to other services

You will see $10,000 quoted online as the STAR+PLUS home modification limit. It is not. The official number is in the STAR+PLUS Handbook, and the $10,000 figures live in two different places.

The 90-business-day completion rule

Section 6650 of the handbook says the MCO must ensure the minor home modification is completed within 90 business days after the ISP start date, or after the ISP revision date when the service is added later. Any delay must be documented, and the member must be told the reason and given a new proposed completion date; each further delay needs the same documentation and notice. Ask your service coordinator for the date the clock started and write it down. If a project is dragging, that section is the one to quote.

Who decides

You do not apply to us, and you do not apply directly to the state. The member's health plan decides whether a modification is part of the service plan, usually after an assessment of needs at home. An occupational therapist or nurse may be involved. The plan typically arranges or approves the contractor and may ask for bids.

How to start

  1. Call the member services number on the STAR+PLUS ID card and ask for your service coordinator.
  2. Describe the problem, not the product: "I can't step over the tub wall safely" or "my caregiver can't help me bathe in this space."
  3. Ask about an assessment and what documentation the plan needs.
  4. Ask what kinds of changes the plan has approved before. A grab bar and a hand shower, a curbless shower, or a tub replacement may be treated differently.
  5. Get approval in writing before any work begins.

Is a walk-in tub the right request?

With a $7,500 lifetime limit, it is worth thinking about the whole picture. A plan may prefer lower-cost changes first, such as grab bars, a transfer bench and a handheld shower, or a shower conversion that also helps a caregiver. If a walk-in tub is the right fit, an itemized quote helps the plan review it. We can provide one; whether the plan pays is up to the plan.

Not on STAR+PLUS?

Texas has other Medicaid waiver programs with their own rules, and many people are not on Medicaid at all. The 28 Area Agencies on Aging and the Aging and Disability Resource Centers (1-855-937-2372) can explain options. TDHCA's Amy Young Barrier Removal Program is another route for households with a person with a disability and limited income. See all Texas funding routes, or answer a few questions in our Texas bathroom funding checker to see which ones fit.

Tips for a smoother request

  • Keep a short diary for a week of what goes wrong at bath time. Concrete examples help an assessor.
  • Ask the doctor to note fall history, balance problems or a recent surgery in the medical record.
  • Ask whether the plan uses its own contractors or accepts outside bids, and how many.
  • If a request is denied, ask for the reason in writing and how to appeal.

Common questions

What is the STAR+PLUS limit for minor home modifications?

The STAR+PLUS Handbook (section 6630, revision 20-2) sets a lifetime limit of $7,500 per member, with $300 per year for repairs, replacement or additional modifications after the limit is reached. That did not change in 2026. The $10,000 figure seen online is the CLASS waiver lifetime limit or the separate STAR+PLUS adaptive aids annual limit, not the home modification cap.

Who approves a STAR+PLUS home modification?

The member's health plan (MCO), usually through a service coordinator after a needs assessment.

Can STAR+PLUS pay for a walk-in tub?

Bathroom modifications are a recognized category, but each request is decided by the health plan based on the member's needs and the program rules. Ask before any work begins.

How long does the plan have to finish a minor home modification?

Handbook section 6650 requires the MCO to ensure completion within 90 business days after the ISP start date, or the ISP revision date if the service was added later. If it runs long, the plan must document the delay and tell you why and when it now expects to finish. Ask your service coordinator for the start date.

I was in a Medicare-Medicaid Plan. Where do I ask now?

The Dual Demonstration ended Dec. 31, 2025. On Jan. 1, 2026 those plans became Integrated D-SNPs in Bexar, Dallas, El Paso, Harris and Hidalgo counties, and Medicaid long-term services now come through the related STAR+PLUS plan. Call the member services number on your STAR+PLUS card and ask for the service coordinator.

Did handbook revision 26-3 change the home modification rules?

No. Revision 26-3, effective Aug. 14, 2026, applies to the STAR+PLUS Program Support Unit Operational Procedures Handbook and covers eligibility, notification and hearing procedures. Section 6600 of the STAR+PLUS Handbook, which holds the $7,500 limit and the 90-business-day rule, was not revised.
