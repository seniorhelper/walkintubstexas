Beyond the metros

Walk-in tubs for rural Texas homes

Texas has a lot of country between its cities. Getting a safer tub out there is very doable with the right plan.

Two ways it usually works

For homes far from the metros, Aging Safely Baths can often ship a tub supply-only, with phone tech support, for a local licensed plumber to install. Coordinated installation may be available in some areas; call to ask about yours. We would rather tell you honestly what is possible at your address than overpromise.

Finding a local installer

  • Ask the plumber who services your well pump or water heater. They already know your system.
  • Check the plumber's license on the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners site, and any electrician's license with TDLR.
  • Share the tub's install guide and spec sheet before you order, so they can confirm the drain side, door direction and electrical needs.
  • Ask how they will remove and dispose of the old tub.

Water wells

Well systems vary. Before you choose a large tub, find out:

  • Pressure and flow. A slow fill means more time sitting in an empty tub. A larger faucet does not help if the well cannot deliver.
  • Sediment and hardness. Sand and minerals can affect valves, seals and jets. A sediment filter or softener may already be in place; if not, ask your well service.
  • Water heater size. Many rural homes have one tank serving everything. A deep tub may need a larger heater or an in-line heater.

Septic systems

A walk-in tub sends a large volume of water to the drain at once. A properly working septic system handles normal household use, and most handle a bath without trouble. If yours is older, undersized, or already slow, talk with your septic service before adding a large tub, and avoid running the dishwasher and washer at the same time as a bath drain.

Power

Older rural homes may have limited electrical panels. Jets and heaters often need dedicated circuits. A soaking tub with no electrical needs is a strong choice when panel space is tight or power is unreliable.

Delivery practicalities

  • Long gravel drives, cattle guards and steep approaches matter for freight trucks. Tell us when you order.
  • Someone must be home to receive and inspect the shipment.
  • Store the tub flat, dry and protected until install day.

Local help in rural counties

Area Agencies on Aging give priority to older adults in rural areas. Call 1-800-252-9240 to reach yours. 2-1-1 Texas lists local programs, and veterans can reach the nearest VA clinic through the VA facility locator.

Questions to ask before you order

  1. What is my well's flow rate, and how long would the tub take to fill?
  2. Is my water heater large enough, and is it gas or electric?
  3. Does my septic system have any known problems?
  4. Is there room on my electrical panel for new circuits if I want jets?
  5. Can a freight truck reach my door, or should delivery go to a nearby address?
  6. Who will haul away the old tub?

Answering these before the order avoids most surprises on install day.

Common questions

Can I get a walk-in tub delivered to a rural Texas address?

Supply-only tubs can often ship across Texas, with phone tech support for your local plumber. Call to ask about installed service in your area.

Will a walk-in tub work with a well?

Usually, but check pressure, flow and sediment, and make sure the water heater can fill the tub.

Will a walk-in tub overload my septic system?

A healthy system typically handles a bath. If yours is older or slow, ask your septic service first.
